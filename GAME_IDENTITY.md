# Percent Pulse · v2

The owner's play feedback prompted a full loop rebuild on 24 October 2026. The generated opening, combat and clear mockups were saved and committed before implementation. [The rebuild plan](docs/rebuild-v2/PLAN.md) supersedes the original five-encounter ammunition/survival design.

Core verbs: move, dodge, Pulse, clear, continue. Move with WASD/arrows, click the floor, or use the touch stick. The blaster selects a marked enemy and fires ordinary 18HP shots automatically. Press Space or the one large Pulse button to remove 25% of every nearby enemy's current HP, interrupt its warning and push it away. No manual aim, weapon switching, dash, boost or ammunition trap is part of this player flow.

The first ten seconds teach movement alone, then show automatic shots and an immediate first clear. The next wave safely waits for the player's first Pulse: a 96HP anchor becomes 72HP, and three 24HP skitters become 18HP. Ordinary fire resumes after the receipt has had time to appear. The changing percentage base remains exact, but the main action choice is when and where to catch threats with a useful shove; this is not framed as a deep optimal-strategy puzzle.

Eight short formations share one compact real 3D arena. Later waves mix two enemy families and end with a larger sentinel. Every damaging attack has a warm floor warning before it resolves. Touching an enemy alone causes no damage. Five visible shield segments, generous hit invulnerability, a segment healed on clear and current-wave retry support recovery. The true final kill clears immediately; no empty-arena survival timer remains. Ordinary fire is unlimited and can finish enemies after both Pulse charges are spent.

State: exact rational enemy HP, player position and shields, stable auto-target, two per-wave Pulse charges from wave two, warning/cooldown state, staged arrivals, local action receipts and retries. Win clears the eighth formation. A clean adaptive browser run took 114.85 active seconds; the intended slice is roughly two to four minutes depending on movement and Pulse use, with no artificial duration floor. Human play is needed to judge the feel.

Camera and art: a close 55° elevated view, big cyan armored player, hot magenta skitters and an existing Chrome-Tripo faceted anchor, on a quiet slate stone ring with a clear cyan edge. Amber warnings, recoil, shove, shards and an expanding cyan Pulse provide action feedback. Reduced effects suppress decorative movement and flashes. Synthesized cues, local licensed fonts and the bundled GLB need no remote service. Generated mockups are references; the actual scene is geometry and live HTML.

Portfolio identity remains an arena action game. It does not borrow the other games' first-person clearance, grapple traversal, area allocation or operation-card duel, nor the canceled courier loop. The lead owns gameplay/integration/Git, the visual contributor owns scene/model provenance and visual comparison, and an independent reviewer owns math, controls and recovery checks.
